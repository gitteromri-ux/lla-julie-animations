# Julie Gibson Clark, Longevity Life Academy
## Opening Buildup Storyboard Content Spec

**Total running time:** 00:00.0 to 00:20.8, 20.8 seconds.

**Creative premise:** A quiet, evidence-led reveal. Julie’s first-person claim creates immediate tension, verified numbers make it credible, the accessible cost makes it relevant, and her lived identity makes the invitation feel earned. This is the opening buildup of a longer masterclass film, not a complete offer explanation.

**CMO lock:**

- **Audience and awareness:** Health-curious adults who know longevity claims exist, but suspect they are reserved for wealthy technologists.
- **Single belief to install:** A measured, evidence-led longevity practice can be personally relevant and financially realistic.
- **CLAIM DISCIPLINE:** The No. 2 rank is a held rank from the 2023 leaderboard, before the rule change. Every card and every spoken line is written in the past tense on purpose. A live check of the leaderboard on 6 August 2026 does not show her in the visible top 25, so present tense would be indefensible.
- **Proof assets named in this opening:** Julie’s 0.665 DunedinPACE result, the 1.00 average benchmark, 33.5 percent slower pace of aging, #2 Rejuvenation Olympics ranking, and roughly $100 monthly cost.
- **Offer cue:** Julie joins the live teaching faculty of eTeacher’s Longevity Life Academy.
- **Format:** Shoot with a protected central portrait composition for both 9:16 and 16:9 finishing.

## SCENE 1, The claim, 00:00.0 to 00:03.9

### THE FEELING
A stranger feels a brief stop of disbelief, then leans in because the claim is calm rather than promotional.

### SHOT
Julie, direct to camera, framed in a tight chest-up portrait with her eyes just above center. Use an 85 mm lens at T1.8, camera nearly locked with a 3 percent controlled push-in over the shot. Keep her irises sharp and the studio background fully softened. Place a soft, large key 45 degrees camera left, a narrow cool rim from rear camera right, and one dim amber practical far behind her. Palette is charcoal, midnight navy, skin warmth, and a restrained amber pinprick. No logo, no lower third, no introductory slate.

### ON-SCREEN TYPE
Card 1, `33.5% slower than average`, centered at lower middle, large condensed white grotesque, 88 percent opacity black gradient behind only the baseline area. Fade in over 6 frames on the word `slower`, hold through the end of the scene.

### VO / SYNC LINE
I age thirty-three point five percent slower than average.

### B-ROLL AT WORD
At the first syllable of `slower`, cut for 10 frames to a macro of Julie’s eye catching the studio key light, then return to her direct gaze before the final word `average` completes. The eye reflection makes the claim feel observed, not announced.

### SOUND
Begin with one low, close cello harmonic and a nearly inaudible room tone. Add a single soft sub hit exactly at `33.5`. Dialogue is fully dominant, dry, intimate, and close-miked. No percussion yet.

### TRANSITION OUT
Match cut from the circular catchlight in Julie’s eye to the circular assay marker on a printed DunedinPACE report. The shared circle motivates the cut and turns the claim into evidence.

## SCENE 2, The number, 00:03.9 to 00:09.2

Light the document with a soft neutral key from overhead and camera left, give Julie a cool rim, and let a shaded window practical create a soft vertical highlight. Palette is paper cream, graphite, desaturated blue, and muted green.

### ON-SCREEN TYPE

### VO / SYNC LINE

### B-ROLL AT WORD

At `Average`, cut to a clean side-by-side graphic in the same paper texture, 0.665 above 1.00, with the lower figure aligned to the real report marker. ### TRANSITION OUT

## WHY THIS BEATS A COLD OPEN ON A LOGO

- Julie’s own face and measured claim give the first second a human stake, before the viewer has any reason to care about a brand mark.
- The sequence earns the Academy identification by moving from result, to evidence, to affordability, to teaching authority.
- Holding the brand until the final release makes the end card feel like the destination of the proof, not the interruption before it.

## MUSIC BRIEF

- **Tempo:** Begin free-time for 3.4 seconds, then establish a restrained 82 BPM pulse at Scene 3.
- **Instrumentation:** Close cello harmonics, low analog bass, brushed hand percussion, muted piano octaves, and a small string ensemble. No trailer booms, no medical sounds, no inspirational pop beat.
- **Dynamic path:** Sparse in Scene 1, measured and analytical in Scene 2, practical pulse in Scene 3, human warmth and upward strings in Scene 4, then a single resolved chord in Scene 5.
- **Dropout:** Pull score and all synthetic texture for the first 6 frames of the child-hands image in Scene 4. Preserve only natural room tone there.
- **Climax:** The first full chord arrives on `choose` in Scene 5 and sustains across the 0.8-second Academy end-card hold.
- **Dialogue relationship:** Keep Julie 5 to 7 dB above the score in Scenes 1 through 4. In Scene 5, dialogue remains dominant until the final consonant of `choose`, then music may rise 1 to 2 dB above the dialogue bed during the silent end-card hold. Finish mix target is minus 14 LUFS integrated, true peak no higher than minus 1 dBTP.

## CAPTION AND SAFE-ZONE SPEC FOR 9:16 AND 16:9

### Caption system

- Set captions in a bold, high-legibility sans serif, white text with a 4 px charcoal stroke and a 60 percent charcoal shadow plate only when footage contrast requires it.
- Caption Julie word-for-word. Break at natural breath points only. Maximum two lines. Keep the voice caption distinct from the designed proof cards. Do not duplicate a proof card word-for-word within the same frame.
- Use sentence case. Place no more than six spoken words per caption line. Keep captions static for the duration of the phrase, with a subtle 3-frame opacity in and out rather than kinetic word highlighting.
- When a designed proof card shares the lower frame, move captions to the upper protected caption band for that scene. Use the same upper band across the whole scene, not just on one frame.

### 9:16 master, 2160 by 3840

- Protect the central 1240 px width for Julie’s face, hands, notebook, report number, and all essential action. Compose every 16:9 source so a 9:16 crop can retain this center action without moving the crop.
- Keep essential on-screen type within x 140 to 2020 and y 300 to 3280. Keep face and eye line within y 640 to 2180 where possible.
- Reserve y 2650 to 3170 for bottom captions when no lower proof card is present. When a lower proof card is present, use the upper caption band y 430 to 870.
- Do not place essential type in the top 300 px or bottom 560 px, which protects against platform interface overlays and device crop variation.
- In Scene 5, build the end card in the central 1640 by 1680 area so both Julie’s name and Academy name remain readable after short-form platform crops.

### 16:9 master, 3840 by 2160

- Keep essential on-screen type within x 220 to 3620 and y 160 to 1900. Maintain a central protection column x 1300 to 2540 for the vertical crop.
- Reserve y 1650 to 1940 for bottom captions when no lower proof card is present. When a lower proof card is present, use the upper caption band y 220 to 500.
- Keep Julie’s eyes near the upper third at y 620 to 780, with at least 18 percent negative space on the opposite side for proof cards.
- Never place proof figures over Julie’s eyes, mouth, or hands. The 0.665 figure must be large enough to read at a 25 percent monitor preview.
